                     ECO Unit Queue Management System
                                             Staff Web Portal
                    UI Screens — Actor, Purpose, Functions and Requirement Mapping

This document catalogues every implemented screen of the Staff Web Portal for the ECO (Echocardiography)
unit queue-management system described in the accompanying research proposal (General Hospital, Sri Lanka).
For each screen it identifies the primary actor (user role), the screen’s purpose, its key functional elements, and
how it maps to the functional requirements (FR1–FR12, Table 3.1) and operational problems identified in
Chapter 1 of the proposal.

Primary actor for this portal: Staff — front-desk/registration clerks, nursing staff, and the medical
officers/technicians performing echocardiograms in the ECO unit.
Screens documented: 11 (Staff Sign In, Forgot/Reset Password, Today’s Overview, Queue Management, Queue
Prediction, Patient Search, Patient Detail, Reports, Register New Patient modal, My Profile, Edit Profile).
Screen 1. Staff Sign In




Actor: Staff (front-desk/registration staff, nurses, ECHO unit technicians, and medical officers) — any authorised
ECO unit staff member
Purpose
This is the entry point of the Staff Web Portal. It authenticates hospital staff before granting access to any
patient data, queue controls, or reporting tools, so that the system is used only by verified ECO unit personnel.
Key functions / elements
 • Staff ID and password fields, with a show/hide toggle on the password field
 • “Keep me signed in” checkbox for trusted devices
 • “Forgot password?” link that opens the password-reset flow (Screen 2)
 • Primary “Sign in” action button plus an alternative “Sign in with hospital SSO” option
 • A visible security notice (“Access is logged and restricted to authorised staff”) reinforcing accountability
 • A branded panel summarising what the portal offers (live queue control, patient intake records, scan
     scheduling) together with live-style indicator cards (staff accounts, patients in queue, uptime)
Relation to requirements / research proposal
Supports the non-functional security and data-privacy requirements described in the methodology (Section
3.10): access to patient data must be restricted and every sign-in/reset event is logged for audit purposes,
feeding into the Audit Log maintained on the Administrator Web Portal. It is the gatekeeper for every functional
requirement (FR1–FR12) that follows, since none of the staff-side features can be reached without
authentication.
Screen 2. Forgot Password / Reset Password




Actor: Staff (any registered user who has lost access to their account)
Purpose
Provides a self-service account-recovery path so that a staff member who cannot sign in is not permanently
locked out, while keeping the recovery process secure and auditable.
Key functions / elements
 • Explanatory panel describing that resets are only ever sent to the verified email on file for the entered Staff
     ID
 • Staff ID input field to identify the account requiring recovery
 • Inline notice that the reset link is single-use and expires after 15 minutes
 • “Send reset link” primary action and a “Back to sign in” link to return to Screen 1
 • A footer notice confirming that password resets are logged for audit and security review
Relation to requirements / research proposal
Directly implements the security and account-governance safeguards discussed in Section 3.10 (Ethical
Considerations) of the proposal, where role-based access, session control, and audit logging are identified as
required controls for any system handling patient-adjacent data under Sri Lanka’s Personal Data Protection Act,
No. 9 of 2022.


Screen 3. Today’s Overview (Dashboard)
Actor: Staff (front-desk/registration staff and ECHO unit technicians) — landing screen after login
Purpose
Gives staff an at-a-glance operational snapshot of the ECO unit the moment they log in, so they can start their
shift with situational awareness instead of walking in blind, directly answering the “lack of real-time monitoring”
problem identified in Chapter 1.
Key functions / elements
 • Four key-performance-indicator cards: patients currently In queue, patients Served today (with day-on-
     day % change), Avg wait (against a target), and the ML-predicted Peak window for the day
 • “Predicted patient inflow (next 6 hrs)” bar chart showing hourly expected patient volume, with the
     current/peak hour highlighted
 • Quick-action buttons: “Go to queue management” and “View prediction detail”, linking directly into Screens
     4 and 5
 • Persistent left-hand navigation (Dashboard, Queue management, Patients, Prediction, Reports) and a
     header with date/time, notifications, and the signed-in staff avatar
Relation to requirements / research proposal
Corresponds to the “Today’s Overview dashboard with key-performance-indicator cards and a patient-volume
chart” listed as a core Staff Web Portal screen in the Scope of the Study (Section 1.5), and is fed by the same ML
Prediction Service (Figure 3.2) that powers the Queue Prediction screen.


Screen 4. Queue Management
Actor: Staff (front-desk staff and ECHO unit technicians operating the live queue)
Purpose
The core operational control surface of the portal: it lets staff run the live ECO queue in real time — calling
patients, skipping them, marking scans complete, and pulling urgent cases forward — replacing the informal,
memory-based ordering used in the manual process.
Key functions / elements
 • Summary strip: Avg time/patient, Longest wait, and Served today
 • A visual visit-stage tracker (Checked in → Now serving → Scanning → Complete) showing where the current
     ticket sits
 • “Now serving” live card showing the active ticket number, patient name, scan type, priority, and elapsed
     time, with Skip and Mark done actions
 • A “Waiting – N patients” list showing each queued ticket with its scan type, priority tag, and estimated wait;
     an URGENT patient is visually flagged in red with a one-tap “Call next” action that jumps them ahead of the
     queue
 • “+ Add walk-in” button in the header to register an unscheduled patient directly into the live queue
Relation to requirements / research proposal
Implements FR3 (“Allow staff to call, skip, or complete tickets and manage the live queue”) and FR4
(“Automatically flag and prioritise urgent/critical patients”) from Table 3.1, and is the staff-side counterpart of
the patient-facing Live Queue Tracking screen — an action taken here (e.g. Mark done) is reflected in real time
on the patient’s mobile app, per the shared REST API architecture in Figure 3.2. It is the design response to the
“no formal queue-numbering system” and “difficulty prioritising urgent cases” problems identified in Chapter 1.


Screen 5. Queue Prediction
Actor: Staff (ECHO unit technicians and shift-planning staff)
Purpose
Surfaces the machine-learning forecasting engine described in the methodology (Section 3.8) directly to staff,
turning historical and live queue data into a forward-looking view of expected demand so that staffing and
pacing decisions can be made proactively rather than reactively.
Key functions / elements
 • Live model status badge (engine version, last-trained time, live indicator)
 • AI Insight banner giving a plain-language recommendation (e.g. inflow trending above average, suggesting
     an extra technician) with a “View reasoning” link
 • KPI cards: patients Expected today, Model confidence, and Next hour forecast
 • “Hourly forecast – next 12 hrs” bar chart comparing Actual vs Forecast patient counts per hour, with the
     busiest/peak hour highlighted
 • “What’s driving this forecast” feature-importance panel (day of week, time of day, historical load, local
     events/holidays) for interpretability
 • Model info panel (version, last trained, 7-day accuracy trend, next scheduled retrain) and an automatic
     retraining-schedule note
Relation to requirements / research proposal
Implements FR5 (“Predict expected patient influx, peak hours, and average wait time using historical/live data”)
and is the direct interface for the Random Forest/XGBoost prediction pipeline described in Section 3.8. The
feature-importance panel operationalises the interpretability requirement identified from the literature (ref.
[14] in Chapter 2), so that staff and administrators can trust and act on the model’s output rather than treating it
as a black box.


Screen 6. Patient Search
Actor: Staff (front-desk/registration staff)
Purpose
Digitises patient look-up and registration, replacing the paper-based patient register that is slow to search,
prone to loss/damage, and vulnerable to duplicate entries.
Key functions / elements
 • Summary cards: Total patients, New this week, Follow-ups due
 • Search bar to find a patient by NIC number or name
 • Filter chips: All, Recent visits, Follow-up due, High priority, plus a “Show: All patients” dropdown
 • “+ New patient” button that opens the Register New Patient modal (Screen 9)
 • A results list showing each patient’s avatar/initials, name, NIC, a colour-coded visit-type tag (e.g. ECHO scan,
     Follow-up, Referral), last-visit date, and an “Open record” button leading to the Patient Detail screen
     (Screen 7)
Relation to requirements / research proposal
Implements the search/registration half of FR6 (“Allow staff to search, view, and register patient records, and
add new patients to the queue”) and directly targets the “manual patient registration” problem from Chapter 1
(records that are time-consuming to search, prone to loss, and vulnerable to duplication).


Screen 7. Patient Detail
Actor: Staff (medical officers/technicians documenting a visit, and front-desk staff reviewing history)
Purpose
Gives staff a full clinical/visit history for a single patient and a place to record the outcome of today’s visit, so
patient information persists digitally instead of being scattered across paper files.
Key functions / elements
 • Patient header: avatar/initials, name, NIC, age, total-visits badge, and a status tag (e.g. Stable – low risk)
 • “Past visits” timeline listing each prior visit with its date, type, a short clinical summary, and a status badge
     (e.g. Normal, Review due)
 • “Add today’s consultation note” field for findings/diagnosis/follow-up plan, with a “Save note” action that
     auto-saves to the patient’s record
 • Breadcrumb navigation back to the Patient Search list
Relation to requirements / research proposal
Implements the record-viewing half of FR6 and operationalises the shift “from paper-based reporting and
absence of historical analysis” toward a searchable digital history, directly supporting continuity of care for
repeat ECO patients.


Screen 8. Reports
Actor: Staff (shift leads / ECHO unit technicians compiling operational reports)
Purpose
Replaces manual, paper-compiled monthly/weekly performance reporting with an on-demand, exportable
operational reporting screen, enabling evidence-based rather than reactive planning.
Key functions / elements
 • Period selector (e.g. “This week”)
 • AI Summary banner highlighting a notable trend (e.g. volume up 12% on last week, driven by specific days)
     with a “View trends” link
 • KPI cards: Total patients (with week-on-week change), Avg wait time (with improvement indicator), and live
     Prediction accuracy
 • “Patients served per day” bar chart (Mon–Fri) with the busiest day highlighted and an average reference
     line
 • “Export as CSV” and “Export full report (PDF)” actions
Relation to requirements / research proposal
Implements FR7 (“Generate operational performance reports over a selectable period”) on the staff side
(mirrored by the Administrator Portal’s own Reports & Insights screen), and responds to the “paper-based
reporting and absence of historical analysis” problem identified in Chapter 1.


Screen 9. Register New Patient (modal)
Actor: Staff (front-desk/registration staff)
Purpose
A focused overlay form for adding a walk-in or newly referred patient straight into today’s queue, described in
its own subtitle as digitising “the manual sign-in book.”
Key functions / elements
 • Fields: Full name, NIC number, Phone, Date of birth
 • “Reason for visit” dropdown (e.g. Routine ECHO scan)
 • Priority selector toggle: Normal or Urgent
 • “Cancel” and “Save and add to queue” actions — saving both creates the patient record and issues a live
     queue ticket in one step
Relation to requirements / research proposal
Implements FR1 (“Allow a patient to digitally join a queue… and receive a ticket”, here performed on the
patient’s behalf by staff) and the registration half of FR6, and is reachable both from the Patient Search screen
(“+ New patient”) and the Queue Management screen (“+ Add walk-in”). It is the clearest single-screen answer
to the “manual patient registration” and “no formal queue-numbering system” problems in Chapter 1.


Screen 10. My Profile
Actor: Staff (the individual signed-in staff member, e.g. an ECHO Unit Technician)
Purpose
Gives a staff member visibility into their own shift performance and recent actions within the system, and is the
entry point for managing their personal account.
Key functions / elements
 • Profile header: avatar/initials, name, role, hospital, Staff ID badge, and on-shift status, with an “Edit profile”
     button linking to Screen 11
 • Personal KPI cards: Patients served today, Avg time/patient, and Shift hours today
 • “Recent activity” feed — a chronological, self-scoped log of the staff member’s own actions (e.g. marked a
     ticket done, called an urgent patient, added a walk-in, updated a consultation note, checked in for shift)
 • “Today’s shift” progress bar showing shift start/end time, check-in time, and time remaining
 • “Patients served this week” mini bar chart
 • “Change password” and “Notification settings” shortcuts
Relation to requirements / research proposal
A supporting account-management screen that complements the Audit Log and role-based access controls
discussed in Section 3.10: the personal “Recent activity” feed gives each staff member the same kind of user-
attributed action history that, at unit level, feeds into the Administrator Portal’s Audit Log.


Screen 11. Edit Profile
Actor: Staff (the individual signed-in staff member)
Purpose
Lets a staff member update their own personal contact details while keeping administratively controlled fields
locked, enforcing the role-based governance model described in the methodology.
Key functions / elements
 • Profile photo upload with “Remove” and “Upload new” actions
 • Editable personal information: Full name, Phone, Email address, Date of birth
 • Read-only “Work details” block: Staff ID, Role, and Department, explicitly labelled as “managed by hospital
     admin and can’t be edited here”
 • “Cancel” and “Save changes” actions, with a “Last updated” timestamp
Relation to requirements / research proposal
Reinforces the security/permission model described in Section 3.10: identity- and role-defining fields (Staff ID,
Role, Department) can only be changed through the Administrator Web Portal’s Staff Accounts screen, while
staff retain self-service control only over their own contact details — a deliberate separation of concerns
between individual users and system administrators.
