PATIENT (USER) MOBILE APPLICATION
  Screen-by-Screen Interface & Functional Description


  Primary Actor: Patient / End-User of the ECO Unit Patient Portal
     Interface: Patient Mobile Application • 14 implemented screens
     Companion reference document to the system research proposal / thesis
1. Overview
The system proposed in the accompanying research is designed around three coordinated interfaces — a
Patient (User) Mobile Application, a Staff Web Portal, and an Administrator Web Portal — built to manage
patient flow and predict queue behaviour in the Echocardiography (ECO) unit of a Sri Lankan public hospital.
This document focuses exclusively on the Patient Mobile Application, the patient-facing side of that system,
and provides a detailed, screen-by-screen description of its user interface and functionality.
The Patient Mobile Application is used by a single actor — the Patient (also referred to as the End-User or
Registered Patient) — across every screen described below. It is designed to replace manual, paper-based
registration and queue handling with self-service digital registration, machine-learning-based wait-time
prediction, live position tracking, and GPS/QR-verified on-site priority handling. Fourteen implemented screens
are covered: five relate to secure account access, three relate to profile and security management, five form
the core queue journey, and one manages on-site priority verification.
Each entry below states the screen’s purpose, its key interface elements exactly as implemented in the design,
how it functions within the wider system, and the research objective or literature theme it corresponds to in
the study.

2. Screen-by-Screen Descriptions

Screen 1 | Secure Login




Actor: Registered Patient (returning user)
Module / Category: Authentication / Access Module
Purpose
The entry-point screen of the Patient Mobile Application. It allows an already-registered ECO-unit patient to
securely sign in to the portal using their National Identity Card (NIC) number and registered mobile number,
instead of a conventional username/password pair.
Key Interface Elements
    ●     Status badge “SECURE LOGIN” and unit label “ECHOCARDIOGRAPHY UNIT”
    ●     Hospital brand mark (heart-rate/pulse icon), “General Hospital” title and “ECHO Unit · Patient Portal”
          subtitle
    ●     Animated ECG waveform graphic reinforcing the cardiology/ECO context
    ●     “Log in / Register” segmented tab control (Log in selected)
    ●     NIC Number input field with ID-card icon
    ●     Phone Number input field, pre-formatted for Sri Lankan mobile numbers (+94)
    ●     Gradient “Continue” call-to-action button
    ●     “New patient? Register with your NIC in one step” shortcut link
    ●     Footer legend (HR / SpO2 / Verified) and a language selector (EN)
Functionality & System Behaviour
On submission, the entered NIC and phone number are validated against the patient record; a successful
match triggers an SMS one-time-password sent to the registered number and routes the patient to the Verify
OTP screen. This NIC-based login removes the need to remember a separate password, reduces duplicate
patient records, and directly supports the study’s objective of replacing manual, paper-based registration at
the ECO unit with a secure digital entry point.
Research Link
Supports Research Objective 2 (digital registration module) and addresses research gap G1 identified in Chapter
2 of the study.


Screen 2 | New Patient Registration




Actor: First-time / walk-in Patient
Module / Category: Authentication / Access Module
Purpose
A self-service sign-up screen that lets a first-time patient create their ECO-unit portal account directly from
their phone, without needing a staff member to manually enter their details at the counter.
Key Interface Elements
    ●     Status badge “NEW PATIENT” and unit label
    ●     “Log in / Register” tab control (Register selected)
    ●     Full Name input field
    ●     NIC Number input field
    ●     Phone Number input field (+94 format)
    ●     Date of Birth input field (DD / MM / YYYY)
    ●     Gradient “Create account →” button
    ●     “Already registered? Log in with your NIC instead” link
Functionality & System Behaviour
Captures the minimum demographic data set (name, NIC, phone, date of birth) needed to create a unique
patient record tied to the NIC number, preventing duplicate or misplaced records that are common under
manual registration. Submitting the form triggers the same OTP verification flow (Screen 3) as login, confirming
the patient controls the phone number before the account is activated.
Research Link
Directly operationalises Research Objective 2 — the digital patient-registration module — and reduces the
administrative overhead identified in the Chapter 1 problem statement.


Screen 3 | Verify OTP




Actor: Patient (mid login or registration)
Module / Category: Authentication / Access Module
Purpose
A two-factor security checkpoint that confirms the patient genuinely controls the mobile number they logged
in or registered with, before the app grants access to any queue or personal data.
Key Interface Elements
    ●     Status badge “VERIFY OTP”, message-bubble icon
    ●     “Verify your number” heading and “Code sent to +94 71 234 5678” confirmation text
    ●     Six-digit segmented OTP input boxes
    ●     “Didn’t get a code? Resend in 00:28” countdown / resend control
    ●     Gradient “Verify & continue →” button
    ●     “Wrong number? Edit phone number” escape link
Functionality & System Behaviour
Validates the time-limited one-time code sent by SMS. This protects patient accounts from unauthorised
access and, because the verified number becomes the channel for all later alerts, guarantees that queue-
position and check-in notifications (see Screen 14) reliably reach the correct patient.
Research Link
Security control underpinning Objectives 2 and 5; mirrors the SMS-based verification pattern reported in the
local OPD-PMMAS and Doc990 literature reviewed in Chapter 2.


Screen 4 | Verification Successful




Actor: Patient
Module / Category: Authentication / Access Module
Purpose
A short confirmation screen that closes the login/registration/OTP flow, reassuring the patient that their phone
number has been verified and their account is ready to use.
Key Interface Elements
    ●     Status badge “VERIFIED” and checkmark icon
    ●     “Verification successful” heading with “General Hospital · Echo Unit” subtitle
    ●     Confirmation message: “Your phone number has been verified and your account is ready to use.”
    ●     Gradient “Back to login →” button
Functionality & System Behaviour
Provides positive, unambiguous closure to the authentication sequence rather than silently redirecting the
patient, which reduces confusion for first-time or less digitally confident users. The button returns the patient
to the Secure Login screen (or forward into the Home Dashboard, depending on the entry flow) so they can
proceed to use the queue features.
Research Link
Usability safeguard supporting the digital-literacy considerations discussed in Chapter 5 (Section 5.9) of the
study.


Screen 5 | Update Phone Number




Actor: Patient
Module / Category: Profile & Security Module
Purpose
A dedicated security screen that lets a patient change the mobile number linked to their account — for
example after switching SIM cards — since that number is the primary channel for OTP login and live queue
alerts.
Key Interface Elements
    ●     Status badge “EDIT DETAILS”, phone icon, “Update phone number” heading, “Patient Portal · Security”
          subtitle
    ●     Explanatory text: “We’ll text a one-time code to confirm any changes to this number.”
    ●     Read-only “Current number” field
    ●     Editable “New phone number” field
    ●     Gradient “Send verification code →” button
    ●     “Cancel and go back” link
Functionality & System Behaviour
Any change to the registered number must be re-confirmed by OTP before it takes effect, preventing an
unauthorised party from silently hijacking the account and intercepting queue notifications. This screen is
reached from the Profile screen’s “Edit details” action.
Research Link
Protects the reliability of the real-time notification channel used by Screens 6, 9 and 14.


Screen 6 | Home Dashboard




Actor: Patient (logged in)
Module / Category: Core Patient Journey
Purpose
The patient’s central landing screen after login. It summarises the patient’s current ticket, the machine-
learning-predicted wait time, and provides one-tap access to every other core action, so the patient never has
to ask staff “how much longer?”
Key Interface Elements
    ●     Personalised gradient header “Hello, Kasun” with avatar initials (KP) and a notification bell showing an
          unread indicator
    ●     “Your queue number” card (e.g. A-014)
    ●     ML-generated “Predicted wait” badge (e.g. 32 min) in an amber gradient card
    ●     “LIVE · Currently serving” card showing the ticket currently being attended (A-009), “5 patients ahead
          of you” and a dot-progress indicator (“of 14”)
    ●     Primary gradient “Join today’s queue →” button
    ●     Secondary “Visit history” and “Profile” quick-access buttons
    ●     “At the hospital now?” priority callout explaining that on-site patients get priority in the queue, with a
          “Show check-in QR” button
    ●     Bottom navigation bar: Home / Queue / History / Profile
Functionality & System Behaviour
Surfaces the output of the system’s wait-time prediction model (Random Forest / XGBoost, Section 3.8 of the
thesis) and the live queue position as an always-available, self-service data point on the patient’s own device,
replacing tacit, staff-mediated updates. It is also the launch point for joining the queue and for the on-site QR
check-in that unlocks priority handling.
Research Link
Central implementation of Research Objectives 2, 4 and 5 (registration, ML wait-time prediction and real-time
status communication).


Screen 7 | Join Today’s Queue




Actor: Patient
Module / Category: Core Patient Journey
Purpose
The digital ticket-request screen where a patient selects their reason for visiting and a preferred time slot,
generating a live queue ticket without needing to visit the registration counter first — the digital replacement
for the paper queue slip.
Key Interface Elements
    ●     Back navigation and “Join today’s queue” title
    ●     “Reason for visit” dropdown selector (e.g. “Routine ECHO scan”)
    ●     “Preferred time slot” chip selector (9–11 AM / 11–1 PM / 2–4 PM)
    ●     “Notes for staff (optional)” free-text field for symptoms or referral details
    ●     ML-driven “Estimated wait for this slot: 40 minutes” callout
    ●     Gradient “Confirm and join queue” button
    ●     Reassurance note: “You can leave the waiting room — we’ll track your place automatically.”
Functionality & System Behaviour
Submitting the form creates a new digital queue ticket and feeds the selected service type, time slot and notes
into the queue-prediction pipeline, which returns an estimated wait time for that specific slot before the
patient commits. This lets patients register and effectively “queue remotely” instead of waiting physically at
the unit from the moment they arrive.
Research Link
Implements Research Objective 2 (automated queue-ticket generation) and the department/service selector
referenced in the Chapter 5 discussion of the Patient Mobile Application.


Screen 8 | Live Ticket (“Your Number”)




Actor: Patient
Module / Category: Core Patient Journey
Purpose
An immediate confirmation screen shown right after a ticket is issued, giving the patient their official queue
number and a live snapshot of the queue.
Key Interface Elements
    ●     Gradient header card “Your number” with a “LIVE UPDATES” badge and the large assigned ticket
          number (e.g. A-014)
    ●     “Predicted: 32 min” badge
    ●     “NOW SERVING” card with the currently-called ticket (A-009) and a visual progress bar
    ●     “5 ahead of you” and “14 in queue total” counters
    ●     “We’ll notify you — 3 patients before your turn” alert
    ●     “Leave queue” (secondary) and “Live Queue” (primary) action buttons
Functionality & System Behaviour
Confirms that the ticket has been successfully created and re-states the ML-predicted wait time so the patient
has an immediate expectation to plan around. The “Live Queue” button takes the patient to the full, staged
Live Queue Tracking screen (Screen 9) for a more detailed view.
Research Link
Bridges Research Objectives 2 and 5, connecting ticket issuance to real-time tracking.


Screen 9 | Live Queue Tracking




Actor: Patient
Module / Category: Core Patient Journey
Purpose
The most detailed real-time screen in the application: a colour-coded, stage-by-stage timeline that shows
exactly where the patient sits in the queue, continuously refreshed by the prediction engine, and transparently
explains why their position may have changed.
Key Interface Elements
    ●     “Live queue tracking” title with an “AI PREDICTING” status badge
    ●     Queue-number card (e.g. A042) with a circular “People ahead” counter (11) and a “Your status: In
          Queue” tag
    ●     Amber “Your position moved back” alert explaining that another patient checked in on-site (QR
          verified) and was given priority
    ●     “Checked in on-site? Show your QR” shortcut button linking to Screen 13
    ●     Vertical staged timeline of surrounding tickets, each with a colour-coded status icon: completed (green
          check, e.g. A039), in progress (pulsing dot, e.g. A040, “~4 min ahead of you”), next up (staff icon, e.g.
          A041, “~7 min”), and the patient’s own ticket highlighted as “YOU ARE HERE” (e.g. A042, “~11 min”)
    ●     Bottom navigation bar
Functionality & System Behaviour
This screen is the direct interface implementation of the system’s priority-based queue-management logic: it
does not just show a number, it visually explains that a legitimately prioritised on-site check-in can move a
remote patient’s position back, so the patient understands the change instead of assuming an error. Position
and wait estimates update live from the ML prediction service as tickets are called, served, or reprioritised by
staff on the Queue Management screen of the Staff Portal.
Research Link
Core implementation of Research Objectives 3, 4 and 5 (priority-based queue management, ML wait-
time/volume prediction, real-time tracking) and closes research gap G2 discussed in Chapter 5.

Module / Category: Profile & Security Module
Purpose
A read-only account-summary screen showing the patient’s key personal and account details at a glance, and

Key Interface Elements
    ●   Gradient header card with avatar initials (KP), full name, NIC number and a “Verified” badge

Screen 13 | Check-in QR
Actor: Patient (on arrival at the hospital)
Module / Category: Priority & Verification Module
Purpose
An on-site presence-verification screen shown at the hospital reception or ECO waiting area, used to prove the
patient has physically arrived and to unlock the queue-priority benefit reserved for on-site patients.
Key Interface Elements
    ●     “CHECK-IN QR” badge and “Show this at reception” heading
    ●     Instruction text: “Staff will scan it to confirm you’re on-site”
    ●     Large scannable QR code unique to the patient’s ticket
    ●     “Expires in 04:12” countdown badge and the associated ticket number (A-014)
    ●     “What happens next” info panel: (1) Staff scans this code at reception, (2) Your GPS location is checked
          against the hospital, (3) You’re marked on-site and may move up the queue
    ●     “Refresh code” button
    ●     Bottom navigation bar
Functionality & System Behaviour
Combines a time-limited QR code with GPS geofencing so the system can reliably distinguish patients who are
physically present at the ECO unit from those queueing remotely. A verified on-site check-in feeds directly into
the priority-queue logic visualised on the Live Queue Tracking screen (Screen 9) and referenced on the Home
Dashboard (Screen 6), giving staff and the system a trustworthy trigger for reordering the queue.
Research Link
Directly implements Research Objective 3 (priority-based queue management) and reduces queue-position
disputes referenced in the Chapter 1 problem statement.


Screen 14 | Notifications




Actor: Patient
Module / Category: Core Patient Journey
Purpose
A chronological feed of every system alert relevant to the patient’s current and recent queue journey, so the
patient stays informed without needing to keep the Live Queue Tracking screen open at all times.
Key Interface Elements
    ●     “Notifications” title and subtitle “Updates about your place in the queue”
    ●     “Your position moved back” alert (priority-reorder notice, e.g. 2 min ago)
    ●     “You’re 3 patients away” proximity alert (e.g. 6 min ago)
    ●     “Checked in successfully” GPS-verified confirmation (e.g. 34 min ago)
    ●     “Queue slot confirmed” ticket-joined confirmation (e.g. 1 hr ago)
    ●     “Appointment reminder” (e.g. Yesterday)
    ●     Bottom navigation bar
Functionality & System Behaviour
Implements the push-style, always-available communication channel that the study identifies as central to
reducing patients’ need to repeatedly ask staff about their turn. Unread items are flagged with a red dot and
mirrored by the notification bell badge on the Home Dashboard, ensuring priority changes and check-in
confirmations are never missed.
Research Link
Directly supports Research Objective 5 (real-time queue tracking and status communication) and mirrors the
SMS-alert pattern documented in the OPD-PMMAS prototype reviewed in Chapter 2.
